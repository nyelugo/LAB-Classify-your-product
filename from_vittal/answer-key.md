# Answer Key — Reveal for Ugo

**My four authored cases**, as sent to Ugo for blind review in [`consulting/vittal-briefs-for-ugo.md`](../consulting/vittal-briefs-for-ugo.md).

| Case | Client brief for partner | Intended category | Why I chose it |
|---|---|---|---|
| 1 | Regional logistics operator — workplace emotion-monitoring tool | **Prohibited** | Article 5(1)(f) bans AI inferring emotions in the workplace and education, except narrow medical/safety exceptions. The brief uses it for performance and disciplinary decisions, which squarely triggers the ban — not an exception. |
| 2 | National retail chain — CV screening / candidate ranking tool | **High-risk** | Annex III(4) lists AI used for recruitment, screening/filtering applications, and evaluating candidates as high-risk. Employment access is a protected area with real consequences for individuals. |
| 3 | Consumer electronics retailer — customer support chatbot | **Limited risk / transparency** | The system interacts directly with natural persons in a way that could be mistaken for a human. Article 50 requires disclosure that users are interacting with an AI system; it doesn't make consequential decisions about people, so it doesn't rise to high-risk. |
| 4 | Regional grocery chain — demand forecasting tool | **Minimal risk** | Purely internal, non-personal, operational forecasting (inventory/stock levels) with no profiling of natural persons and no Annex III use case. Falls outside AI Act specific obligations, though GDPR/consumer-protection issues should still be flagged if any personal data creeps in later. |

## Notes for the reveal (Phase 4)

- Case 1 is written to look like a "wellness/engagement" initiative — the danger is intent creep from monitoring into disciplinary use.
- Case 2 is written to look like an efficiency play ("we get 400 CVs a week") to bury the Annex III trigger under an ordinary-sounding HR pain point.
- Case 3 is written to sound almost like Case 4 (both "customer-facing tool") — the tell is that it holds a live, human-like conversation, which is what pulls in the transparency duty.
- Case 4 is written to sound like it "uses AI to make decisions" to bait a high-risk read, but the decisions are about stock units, not people.
