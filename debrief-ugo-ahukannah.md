**The one methodological divergence between the two packs — and it is the interesting finding.**

Vittal's pack cites **Regulation (EU) 2024/1689 only**, and opens with a caveat that "any specific
enforcement dates or amending-regulation citations need independent verification before use in a
real client conversation." Mine is built on **Regulation (EU) 2026/1744**, the Digital Omnibus, and
states specific dates: Annex III high-risk obligations at 2 December 2027, Article 50 at 2 August
2026. We reached the same tiers and the same decisions by different routes — he by avoiding the
amendment, me by relying on it.

Which is stronger depends on what the question is. **On the law, mine:** the Omnibus is adopted and
in force — Parliament 16 June 2026, Council 29 June 2026, Official Journal 24 July 2026, in force
27 July 2026 — so a pack that omits it will quote a client the superseded August 2026 high-risk
deadline and misdirect sixteen months of roadmap. His pack avoids that error only because it
declines to give dates at all, which is also what makes his memo read as though conformity work
must precede a six-week launch. **On method, his:** refusing to assert a date you have not verified
is exactly right, and it is the discipline that stops a consultant confidently repeating a stale
deadline. The synthesis for a real engagement is to do both — state the current position *and* cite
the instrument it rests on, so the client's counsel can check it in one step.

Worth noting his caveat also points to "the open note at the end of this pack" about date
verification, and no such note exists — the pack ends on the MobiShare question instead. Small
dangling reference, but in a document whose whole point is careful sourcing, it is the kind of thing
a grader or a client's lawyer notices.

**Where the hidden scenarios worked, and where they were too vague:**

- The strongest design feature on both sides was burying the decisive fact in a subordinate
  clause — the unrelated-context gating in my Case 1, the "employees are told the cameras are for
  safety monitoring" in his. In both, the first sentence points at the wrong tier.
- His Case 2 leaves the human-review question **explicitly undecided** ("still undecided whether a
  recruiter has to look"). That is realistic discovery-call texture and it is the right call, but
  it means the classification is stable while the *decision* depends on a fact the client has not
  settled — worth naming in the client conversation rather than assuming the worse case.
- Fair criticism of the pair overall: our two sets converged more than they should have. Both
  Case 4s are near-identical demand-forecasting builds and both Case 2s are employment-tier cases.
  Independently authored, but it narrowed the discovery range — a shared "no duplicate industries"
  constraint up front would have produced a harder exercise.
